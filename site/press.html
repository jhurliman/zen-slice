Description

Chord Cut is a slow fruit-slicing game where the blade is the instrument. Every cut plays a piano chord from the current level's palette, and the tempo follows how fast you slice. There is no backing track. The player composes the soundtrack, one cut at a time. Nine timed levels follow a day from Still Water to Night Jasmine, the harmony deepening as the orchard changes, and the tenth, Dreaming of Bliss, is endless, with the full palette.

What makes it interesting

  • Every cut plays a chord. A single fruit plays a tone, two fruits in one swipe play a dyad, three or more play a triad or a full chord, all quantized to a beat inferred from your own tempo.
  • Nothing is baked in. Every fruit, material, juice droplet, light and piano note is generated on the device. The piano is rendered at launch, not sampled. There are no image textures anywhere in the game.
  • Real cutting, real juice. Fruit meshes are split geometrically along the swipe; the juice is a GPU fluid simulation tuned against high-speed photography.
  • One 4 MB file. The whole game ships as a single self-contained HTML file inside a native shell. Nothing is downloaded after install and it plays fully offline. Game Center keeps a top-score leaderboard when you are online.
  • Feels the cut. Haptics are tuned per slice and per change in harmony, and Reduce Motion is respected.
  • Open source. GPLv3, with a public engineering log that records every design decision. As far as we know it is the only paid game on the App Store whose entire source is public.

About the developer

John Hurliman is a solo developer. Chord Cut was built in the open, with its full engineering history, dead ends included, published in the repository's engineering log. The web demo runs at 120 fps on ProMotion displays; the App Store build runs at 60, the ceiling WebKit sets for embedded web views.
